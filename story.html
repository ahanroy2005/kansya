THE SOUL OF
LOST WAX.

Dhokra is not merely a craft; it is a 4,000-year-old dialogue between fire and earth. Emerging from the tribal heartlands of Dhenkanal, Odisha, the ancient lost-wax technique is one of the oldest known methods of non-ferrous metal casting. Today, this endangered lineage is preserved by a few master kaarigars who still mold magic with their bare hands.

"In the world of Loha Mati, there is no master mold. Every artifact is a singular event in time, a technical achievement that exists only once before the mold is shattered."

Technical Archive

Clay Foundation

The process begins with the earth. A solid core is sculpted from locally sourced riverbed clay, mixed with rice husk to withstand the immense heat of the foundry. This rough core dictates the interior volume of the final artifact.

Wax Pattern

Pure beeswax, combined with resin from the sal tree, is drawn into fine wires. The artisan meticulously wraps and weaves these wax threads around the clay core, creating the intricate textures and geometric patterns that define Dhokra art.

Linear Threading

The hallmark of Dhokra lies in its linear detailing. Instead of carving, patterns are built by applying wax threads one by one. Once encased in a final layer of clay and fired, the wax melts away (is "lost"), leaving a void to be filled by molten metal.

PROCESS

The Art of Lost Wax Casting

01

THE CORE SCULPTING

The process begins with gathering clay from local riverbeds. The artisan mixes this clay with rice husk and shapes it into the basic core form of the desired object. This core is then left to dry in the sun.

02

WAX THREADING

Pure beeswax is mixed with resin and nut oil, then extruded through a traditional press to create long, hair-thin threads. The artisan meticulously wraps these threads around the dried clay core, creating the intricate designs, motifs, and textures that define Dhokra art.

03

THE OUTER MOLD

Once the wax design is complete, it is covered with another layer of fine clay, followed by a thicker layer of clay mixed with cow dung and husk. A small channel (runner) is left open at the top. The entire piece is dried again.

04

THE "LOST WAX" FIRING

The clay mold is baked in a traditional wood-fired furnace. As the temperature rises, the wax inside melts and drains out through the channel—hence the term "lost wax". This leaves a perfect hollow cavity inside the baked clay mold, bearing the exact negative impression of the wax design.

05

METAL CASTING

Scrap brass, bronze, and copper are melted in a crucible at temperatures exceeding 1000°C. The glowing, molten metal is carefully poured into the channel of the hot clay mold, filling the void left by the melted wax.

06

THE REVEAL & FINISHING

After cooling, the outer clay shell is broken open to reveal the solidified metal casting. Because the mold must be destroyed, every single Dhokra piece is a unique, unrepeatable original. The piece is then cleaned, polished, and given an antique patina finish.

Modern Brutalism, Ancient Craft.

At KANSYA, we view Dhokra through the lens of modern brutalism. The raw, unpolished textures, the honest expression of materials, and the imposing weight of bell metal resonate deeply with contemporary spatial design. We are not just preserving a craft; we are recontextualizing it for the modern sanctuary.
